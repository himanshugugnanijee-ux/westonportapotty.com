How Many Portable Toilets Do You Need?

A practical planning guide from your Weston, FL portable toilet supplier: guest-count ratios, OSHA job site basics, and the placement details that keep lines short and units clean.

By RapidClean Dispatch May 10, 2026 7 min read

Order too few porta potties and your guests or crew will queue for twenty minutes. Order too many and you pay for units nobody needs. The right number depends on headcount, how long people stay, and what they are doing while they are there. This guide gives you a dependable starting point.

Events: how many units per guest count

For a typical daytime event of about four hours, a good rule of thumb is one standard portable toilet for every 40 to 50 guests. The table below shows common starting points for a general event with no alcohol and a steady flow of guests rather than a single rush.

What changes the number

The guest count is only the starting line. Four factors push the number up:

  • Event length. Events that run past four to six hours need more units, because every guest will visit at least once and many will visit more than once.
  • Alcohol. If beer, wine or cocktails are served, plan on roughly 25 to 50 percent more units than the table suggests.
  • Peaks. Weddings, concerts and races create rushes at intermission, between ceremony and reception, or right after a finish. Size for the peak, not the average.
  • Hot weather. South Florida heat means guests drink more water. Expect more restroom visits at midday summer events.

Pro tip: Split units into two or three small groups in different spots instead of one long row. Guests use the closest restroom, and a second location keeps any single line from backing up.

Construction sites and OSHA basics

Job sites are planned by crew size and regulation, not by guest count. Federal OSHA construction rules (29 CFR 1926.51) set the minimum number of toilets by the number of workers on site:

Many general contractors order above the minimum so crews are not walking across a big site for a restroom break. Choosing the right unit types

Once you know the count, decide what kind of units to rent. Most orders mix several types:

  • Standard portable toilets are the dependable, budget-friendly choice for job sites, yards and casual events.
  • Deluxe flushable restrooms add a flushing toilet and sink, which guests at private parties appreciate.
  • ADA accessible units provide wheelchair-friendly space. Public events should include at least one.
  • Handwashing stations are essential wherever food is served and strongly recommended everywhere else.
  • Luxury restroom trailers are climate-controlled and ideal for weddings, galas and corporate events.

Placement and delivery tips for Florida

Pick a firm, level, reachable spot

Our drivers need a clear path wide enough for the delivery truck and a flat surface for each unit. After a Florida downpour, soft grass can trap a truck, so a driveway, paved lot or compacted ground is best.

Think shade and sightlines

Units that bake in direct afternoon sun get uncomfortable fast. Where you can, place them in partial shade, close enough to be convenient but downwind of food and seating areas.

Light it and sign it

For evening events, add lighting along the path and clear signage so guests can find the units without wandering. Mark the accessible unit with a firm, smooth route to its door.

Plan for service on long rentals

Any rental that runs past a single event day should include scheduled servicing so units stay fresh. RapidClean includes delivery, pickup and weekly service in flat quotes on standard job site rentals.

Quick ordering checklist

  1. Estimate your peak headcount and the length of the event or project.
  2. Use the ratios above, then adjust for alcohol, heat and rush periods.
  3. Add at least one accessible unit for public or permitted events.
  4. Add handwashing stations, especially if food is served.
  5. Pick a delivery spot with truck access and level ground.
  6. Call for a flat quote and lock in your delivery window.

Frequently asked questions

How many porta potties do I need for 100 guests?

For a roughly four-hour daytime event with 100 guests, two to three standard units is a typical starting point. Add units if the event runs longer, serves alcohol, or has a heavy peak when everyone arrives or leaves at once.

Do I need an ADA accessible portable toilet?

Public events and many permitted sites are expected to provide at least one wheelchair-accessible unit. Even at private events, an accessible unit is a considerate choice. Tell us your guest count and venue and we will recommend how many to include.

How many portable toilets does a construction site need?

Federal OSHA construction rules call for one toilet for crews of fewer than 20 workers, and one toilet seat and one urinal per 40 workers for larger crews, stepping to one per 50 once a site passes 200 workers. Confirm local and project-specific requirements with your safety lead.

How far ahead should I book portable toilets in Weston, FL?

Book as early as you know your date, especially for weddings, festivals and holiday weekends. RapidClean also offers same-day delivery across Weston and Broward County when units are available.

Should I add a handwashing station?

Yes for any event with food, and for most job sites. A freshwater handwashing station improves hygiene, keeps lines moving and is often required where food is served.
