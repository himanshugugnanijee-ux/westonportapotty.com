How Many Portable Toilets Do I Need? A Weston, FL Rental Guide

The most common question our dispatchers hear is also the easiest to get wrong. Order too few porta potties and you get lines, complaints and overflowing tanks. Order too many and you are paying for units nobody uses. Here is the exact sizing math we use for events, weddings and job sites across Broward County.

Whether you are planning a backyard wedding in Weston Hills, a youth tournament at Regional Park, a festival in Davie or a three-month commercial build off Bonaventure Boulevard, the number of portable toilets you rent shapes the entire guest or crew experience. The good news: portable toilet sizing is not guesswork. It comes down to a handful of variables — headcount, duration, food and drinks, gender mix, and how often the units will be serviced. Once you know those, the answer is simple.

This guide walks through the same rules of thumb our Weston dispatch team uses every day when quoting porta potty rentals, event restroom rentals and construction site toilets across Broward County. The Basic Rule: One Toilet Per 50 Guests, Per 4 Hours

For most general events, the industry starting point is one standard portable toilet for every 50 guests for an event lasting up to four hours. A standard porta potty holding tank comfortably handles roughly 200 uses before it needs to be pumped, and the average guest uses a restroom about once every two to three hours. That is where the 50-guest benchmark comes from.

As the event gets longer, the math changes. Every additional few hours adds more uses per guest, so the number of units should grow with duration. The table below is the quick reference we use for most parties, community events and private gatherings.

Adjust For Alcohol, Food And Drinks

Beverages are the single biggest factor that pushes restroom demand above the baseline. If alcohol is being served — beer at a tailgate, an open bar at a wedding reception, or a craft beer festival — increase your total by 15 to 20 percent. The same applies to events with heavy soft drink, water or iced tea consumption in the South Florida heat. Summer afternoon events in Weston are noticeably harder on restrooms than winter evening events for exactly this reason.

Any time food is served, we also strongly recommend adding portable handwashing stations. A good guideline is one handwashing station for every two to three toilets, or one per food vendor at festivals. Hand sanitizer inside each unit helps, but guests handling food genuinely appreciate running water and soap.

Account For Gender Mix

Women typically spend longer in a restroom than men, so events with a mostly female audience — bridal showers, quinceañeras, baby showers, ladies’ luncheons and many weddings — should add roughly one extra unit for every 100 guests. For events with a mostly male crowd, adding a portable urinal or a unit with a built-in urinal can reduce lines without adding as many full toilets.

Weddings And Upscale Events

For weddings, galas and corporate events, guest comfort matters as much as capacity. Many hosts in Weston and Southwest Ranches choose deluxe flushable restrooms with a built-in sink, mirror and fresh-water flush, or step up to a climate-controlled luxury restroom trailer. A two-stall trailer generally serves about 100 to 150 guests over an evening, while a four-to-six station trailer can handle 200 to 300 guests. If you are mixing trailers with standard units — for example, a trailer near the reception tent and standard units near the parking area — our team can help balance the layout.

Construction Sites: Follow OSHA

Job sites follow a different rule set. OSHA standard 1926.51 requires toilet facilities based on the number of workers on site. In practice, that means:

  • 1–20 workers: at least 1 toilet
  • 20–199 workers: 1 toilet seat and 1 urinal per 40 workers
  • 200+ workers: 1 toilet seat and 1 urinal per 50 workers

Those are minimums based on a 40-hour work week with weekly servicing. Most contractors in Broward County find that one unit per 10 workers keeps crews happier and productive, especially during Florida’s hot, humid months. Larger crews or longer shifts may need twice-weekly servicing. Don’t Forget ADA Accessible Units

Any public event and most commercial job sites should include at least one ADA accessible portable toilet. The common guideline is that at least 5 percent of your total units — and never fewer than one — should be wheelchair accessible. ADA units have a ground-level entrance, a wider door, interior grab bars and enough turning space for a wheelchair. They are also popular with parents managing strollers or small children, so they rarely go unused.

Long-Term Rentals And Servicing

For rentals lasting longer than a week — farms, storage yards, sports facilities, church grounds or extended remodels — servicing frequency matters as much as the number of units. A unit that is pumped, cleaned and restocked weekly can serve about 10 regular users. If usage is heavier, you can either add a unit or increase service visits. Our long-term and monthly portable toilet rental plans include discounted rates and a fixed service schedule so you never have to think about it.

When in doubt, round up. One extra unit costs far less than a line of unhappy guests.

Placement Tips That Make A Difference

  • Place units on level, firm ground — packed dirt, gravel or pavement work best.
  • Keep them close enough to be convenient but downwind of food and seating areas.
  • Leave clear truck access within about 25 feet so our technicians can service the tanks.
  • Group units in clusters rather than spreading single units around a large site.
  • Add lighting for evening events so guests can find restrooms safely after dark.
  • In hurricane season, ask about anchoring units if storms are in the forecast.

Quick Sizing Checklist

Before you call, jot down these five details and you will have an accurate quote in minutes:

  • Expected peak headcount (guests or workers)
  • Event length or rental duration
  • Whether alcohol or food will be served
  • Ground surface and truck access at the drop-off spot
  • Any accessibility or upscale comfort requirements
